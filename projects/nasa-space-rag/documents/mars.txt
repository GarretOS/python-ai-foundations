Title: Mars
Source: NASA Science — Mars Facts
Source URL: https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun. It is a cold, dusty desert world with a very thin atmosphere, and its rusty surface gives it the familiar name “Red Planet.” Mars has seasons, polar ice caps, extinct volcanoes, canyons, and changing weather. Its day lasts about 24.6 hours, and a Martian year lasts 669.6 sols, or 687 Earth days.

The atmosphere of Mars is made mostly of carbon dioxide, nitrogen, and argon. Because it is so thin, it does not protect the surface well from impacts, and heat escapes easily. Dust suspended in the atmosphere would make the sky look hazy and red. Winds can sometimes raise dust storms that cover much of the planet, and the dust may take months to settle.

NASA missions have found strong evidence that Mars was warmer and wetter billions of years ago. Ancient river valleys, deltas, and lakebeds, along with certain rocks and minerals, indicate that liquid water once existed on the surface. Some features even suggest huge floods about 3.5 billion years ago. Water still exists on Mars today, mainly as ice beneath the surface and near the poles; the thin atmosphere means liquid water cannot remain on the surface for long.
