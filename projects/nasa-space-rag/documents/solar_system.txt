Title: Solar System
Source: NASA Science — Solar System Facts
Source URL: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/solar-system-facts/

Our solar system contains one star, the Sun, eight planets, five officially named dwarf planets, hundreds of moons, and thousands of asteroids and comets. It is part of the Milky Way galaxy. The solar system extends beyond the planets and includes the Kuiper Belt, a ring of icy bodies past Neptune, and the distant Oort Cloud, a predicted spherical shell of icy objects surrounding the system.

The eight planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. The four planets nearest the Sun—Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars—are terrestrial planets with small, solid, rocky surfaces. Farther from the Sun are the gas giants Jupiter and Saturn and the ice giants Uranus and Neptune. Mercury and Venus are the only planets without moons, although Venus has a named quasi-satellite. The solar system also has moons orbiting planets, dwarf planets, and asteroids.

NASA says the solar system formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a dense cloud of interstellar gas and dust. As the cloud collapsed, it became a spinning disk. Material collected at the center to form the Sun, while other clumps grew into planets, dwarf planets, and large moons. Leftover material became objects such as asteroids and comets.
