Title: Moon Phases
Source: NASA Science — Moon Phases
Source URL: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/

The Moon does not make its own light. What we call moonlight is sunlight reflected from the Moon’s surface. The Sun always illuminates half of the Moon, while the other half is dark. As the Moon travels around Earth, the amount of its sunlit half that we can see changes. These changing views are called Moon phases.

There are eight phases in the lunar cycle: new Moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full Moon, waning gibbous, third quarter, and waning crescent. The cycle repeats about every 29.5 days. During a new Moon, the Moon’s illuminated side faces the Sun and its dark side faces Earth, so the Moon is usually not visible. During a full Moon, the Moon is opposite the Sun as seen from Earth, and its day side is turned toward us.

The words waxing and waning describe the apparent change in the lit portion. Waxing phases grow brighter, while waning phases grow less illuminated from our viewpoint. A first-quarter or third-quarter Moon may look half lit, but observers are seeing half of the half that sunlight illuminates. Moonrise and moonset times also change with the phases: a full Moon rises around sunset, while a new Moon rises and sets with the Sun.
